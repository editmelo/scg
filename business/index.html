What this usually looks like

It's rarely a people problem.

Everything routes through you.

Employees can't make a decision without approval, so the owner becomes the answer to every question and the ceiling on every day.

Sales grew. Profit didn't.

More revenue moving through loose pricing and no job costing just moves more money past you faster.

Scheduling is chaos.

Work gets assigned by whoever is loudest or nearest, and nobody can see what's committed until it's late.

You promoted someone into a title.

But you never defined what authority the role actually carries, so the job didn't come off your desk.

Everyone is working hard and the business still feels harder to operate than it should. That's a systems problem.

The premise

Experience is the product.

Stringfellow isn't here to tell business owners how businesses work from a textbook. It's here to solve real operational problems.

Sometimes that's a two-hour conversation. Sometimes it's rebuilding one process. Sometimes it's helping an owner restructure an entire operation for its next stage of growth.

Deliverables

A working solution, not a binder.

Every engagement produces something your team can actually use on Monday. The goal isn't more paperwork — it's a business that doesn't require the owner to personally hold everything together.

Fit

This isn't for everyone.

Saying so up front saves us both an afternoon.

  • Owners who want a plan, not a change. We get inside the operation and rebuild what isn't working. If you want a document that describes your company back to you, that's a different service.
  • Anyone looking for a funding or investor deck. This is operations, systems, and structure — not capital raising.
  • Businesses that already run without the owner. If the systems are in place and the numbers are clean, keep your $227.
  • Anyone who won't change anything. A diagnosis nobody acts on is an expensive conversation. The work only pays off if the structure actually changes.

The calls we get

Most people call us
with one of these.

“Is this quote fair?”

We read estimates for a living. Bring three bids and we'll tell you which one is priced honestly and which one is missing half the scope.

“Why did insurance deny that?”

A denied line item usually means missing documentation or an unaccounted code requirement — not a closed case.

“Why am I the answer to every question?”

When nothing moves without the owner, that's rarely a people problem. It's a systems problem, and systems can be built.

“Sales are up. Why isn't profit?”

Growth exposes weak pricing and loose job costing. We find where the money is actually going before you sell another job at the same rate.

Whichever one you asked, you don't need to hire anybody yet. You need an hour.

The premise

Experience is the product.

Stringfellow isn't here to explain how construction or business works from a textbook. It's here to solve real problems for people who are already in one.

Sometimes that's a two-hour conversation. Sometimes it's rebuilding a single process. Sometimes it's restructuring an entire operation for its next stage of growth.

The engagement gets bigger only when the problem requires it.

That's the whole business model. You are buying judgment and documentation, not hours. If two hours is genuinely all you need, that's what you'll be sold.

Book a consultation — $227

Deliverables

You leave with documents, not opinions.

Every engagement produces something you can hold, hand to a contractor, take to your carrier, or put in front of your team. That is the whole difference between advice and consulting.

On the construction side

  • Written findings and recommendations
  • Budget range with the reasoning behind it
  • Scope of work you can hand to bidders
  • Material list and specifications
  • Trade and inspection schedule
  • Contractor bid review
  • Permit and inspection guidance
  • Written risk assessment

On the business side

  • Written diagnosis of the actual bottleneck
  • Roles and responsibilities, defined
  • Standard operating procedures
  • Scheduling and workflow design
  • Pricing and job-costing procedure
  • Accountability and reporting structure
  • Chain of command and authority
  • Implementation sequence

Process

How the first one goes.

Call or email

Tell us what you're facing in a couple of sentences. We'll say whether a consultation is the right starting point — and we'll say so if it isn't.

Two hours on your problem

Phone, Zoom, or on site. We go through whatever is relevant — estimates, photographs, the property, the numbers, the org chart — and answer everything you ask.

You get it in writing

A written summary: findings, concerns, costs where they apply, and a recommended sequence. What you do with it from there is entirely up to you.

Fit

This isn't for everyone.

Saying so up front saves us both an afternoon.

  • People shopping for the lowest bid. If price is the only variable that matters, you don't need a consultant. You need three quotes and a coin.
  • Anyone looking to hire labor by the hour. We sell judgment and documentation. Tier 04 is the only tier where we build, and we take very few of those.
  • Owners who want a plan, not a change. On the business side we get inside the operation and rebuild what isn't working. If you want a document that describes your company back to you, that's a different service.
  • Small, well-understood problems. A consultation should save you more than it costs. If you already know the scope and the answer, we'll tell you to keep your $227.
  • Anyone who wants their claim negotiated. Negotiating a settlement with a carrier is public adjusting. It's a different license and a different job.
